Make an unscoreable farmer scoreable in minutes.

Smallholder farmers are locked out of credit because they have no formal credit history and no collateral, so lenders treat them as unscoreable and stay away. Shamba Score builds a risk profile from satellite, weather and mobile-money data instead, and returns a score, a default probability, recommended loan terms and a plain-language explanation.

We are not a lender.

Shamba Score is the decisioning layer that licensed institutions use. They hold the risk, the capital and the licence. We sell the brain, not the balance sheet — which is what keeps the heaviest regulation with our customers and makes the product buildable by a small team.

The paying customer is the institution: a microfinance lender, a SACCO, an agri-input dealer or an insurer. It monetises the transaction it can now safely make. The farmer, who would not pay for information, does not have to.

How it works

Three steps, all of which a loan officer can complete on a phone in the field.

1

Capture the farmer and the farm

Name, phone, crop, hectarage, and the farm pinned on a map. Explicit consent is recorded with a timestamp before anything is processed. No national ID and no account numbers are collected, by design.

2

Gather alternative data

Satellite vegetation over the plot, rainfall history for the location, and — where the farmer authorises it — a summary of mobile-money activity. Each source sits behind one interface, so any of them can be swapped or go dark without breaking the rest.

3

Return a decision the officer can defend

A score, a default probability, a recommended amount and term, a confidence level, and the specific factors that drove the result — each in one plain sentence, with the points it contributed.

The signals

Everything runs end to end on mock data today, so the product exists before any partnership or API key does. Sources go live one at a time, behind the same interface.

The two honest gaps

Mobile-money behaviour and real repayment outcomes are not publicly available anywhere. Both are mocked today and become real only through a pilot institution and consented farmers. That is the cold-start reality of this problem, not a shortcut around it.

Explainable enough to underwrite on

At launch there is no labelled repayment data, so there is nothing to train on — a model fitted to zero observed defaults would be a black box that had learnt nothing. The first version is a transparent, expert-weighted model instead: every weight is visible, arguable and tunable, and every score traces back to the inputs that produced it.

  • Missing data is never quietly treated as good news. A gap is filled at no better than a conservative anchor, it lowers the confidence shown to the officer, and it caps the size of any offer.
  • Withholding a signal can never improve the outcome. The risk band never gets better and the recommended amount never gets larger, so there is nothing to gain by hiding your weakest evidence.
  • Never having borrowed is not the same as having borrowed badly. A farmer with no credit history is not scored as though they repaid poorly. That distinction is the whole point of a thin-file model.
  • Every decision is auditable. The full set of inputs is stored alongside the result, with the model version and who decided, so an assessment can be reconstructed months later.
  • A recommendation can be "decide this by hand". When the evidence is too thin or the risk too high, the model declines to suggest terms rather than inventing a number.

Who it is for

Microfinance lenders and SACCOs

Approve a larger share of smallholder applications without loosening standards, and cut assessment from days to minutes.

Agri-input dealers

Run a fast creditworthiness check at the point of sale, so seed and fertiliser can be offered on pay-later terms.

Insurers

Price parametric weather-index cover for smallholders off the same rainfall signals that drive the credit score.

Where the project actually is

Being straight about this matters more than the pitch. Shamba Score is in active development. There is no pilot institution, no live external data source and no lending yet.

  • Built: multi-tenant accounts with database-enforced isolation, farmer and application intake with recorded consent, the pluggable data-adapter layer with mock sources, and the scoring engine with its explanation panel.
  • Next: the approve and decline flow with its audit snapshot, a portfolio view, and the first live data source.
  • Then: repayment-outcome capture, which is what eventually makes a trained model possible.
